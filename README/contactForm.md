Yes — I strongly recommend adding an estimated budget range to your software-company enquiry form.

But I would not phrase it as:

"What is your budget?"

That can make a serious prospect feel like you're trying to extract the maximum amount they can pay.

Instead, make it:

Estimated Project Budget

and explain:

Select the range that best represents what you are prepared to invest. This helps us recommend the right approach and scope.

Why this is smart for your company

It gives you three very useful things before you spend time analysing an enquiry:

Qualification — someone asking for a custom ERP with a KSh 50,000 budget is immediately different from someone with KSh 2M.
Scope alignment — you can determine whether to recommend a website, MVP, custom application, or larger system.
Positioning — you're presenting yourself as a software engineering company that scopes and solves problems, rather than someone who simply "makes websites."

And importantly, don't make the budget field mandatory. Give them:

Not sure yet / I'd like guidance

That captures serious clients who genuinely don't know what their project should cost.

The budget ranges I'd use for your form

I've looked at current 2026 Kenyan pricing. There is a huge spread in the market: published Kenyan estimates put basic websites anywhere from roughly KSh 15k–50k, while professional/custom corporate sites can run KSh 100k–350k+, e-commerce around KSh 100k–500k, and custom web applications from roughly KSh 250k into the millions.

For your particular positioning, I would actually set your ranges somewhat higher than the cheap-freelancer market.

Software

This is where you should stop thinking like a web designer.

A software system isn't priced primarily by the number of pages.

It's priced by:

requirements → architecture → workflows → roles → integrations → database → security → development → testing → deployment → maintenance.

One more important point for your particular website

Given the kind of company website you've been building — showing that you actually engineer systems rather than simply create websites — I would make the form itself demonstrate that capability.

Instead of a basic:

Name → Email → Phone → Message

make it more like a software discovery / project intake form:

01 — What are you trying to build?
02 — What problem are you trying to solve?
03 — Who will use it?
04 — What capabilities do you need?
05 — Does it need integrations?
06 — Estimated project investment?
07 — Desired timeline?
08 — Tell us about the project.

That turns the form into part of your proof of engineering capability.

And that's much more powerful for the positioning you're aiming for than simply asking someone to "contact us."
